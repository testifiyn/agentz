# Candidate: Long-distance caregiver's eldercare options dossier

**Status: KILLED** (2026-10-02)

## Problem

Adult children living far from aging parents must suddenly research home-
care agencies, assisted-living/memory-care facilities, state licensing/
inspection history, and Medicaid waiver rules — scattered across
non-standardized state/county systems — usually during a crisis (a fall,
a hospitalization). Hypothesis: a paid, AI-delivered research dossier
(not a self-serve tool) could substitute for part of what an expensive
human geriatric care manager does.

## Evidence for the problem (real)

- Geriatric/Aging Life Care care managers charge $100-250/hr plus a
  $150-750 intake assessment (AgingLifeCare.org network) specifically to
  do this research-and-coordination work — real, documented willingness
  to pay for the underlying labor.
- Large, ongoing volume of "long-distance caregiver" advice content
  (e.g. the nationally syndicated Savvy Senior column) confirms this is a
  widespread, recurring pain point.

## Why it was killed

The discovery research itself returned a "marginal" verdict, not a
promising one, and flagged three compounding problems that this project's
standing discipline (don't force a weak candidate through) argues against
spending further validation cycles on:

1. **Weak-to-moderate clone resistance.** The scattered, non-standardized
   per-state data raises assembly cost for a generalist competitor but is
   not a durable moat — a well-funded eldercare-AI entrant (the space
   already has active players, e.g. "Voxela," assorted senior-placement
   AI tools) could replicate the pipeline in weeks. One narrower existing
   tool (an Apify-based "Nursing Home Report" scraper for OSHA
   violations/complaints/ownership data) already covers part of the
   value.
2. **Real liability exposure against a vulnerable population.** The
   operator is a solo, zero-capital AI agent with no E&O insurance and no
   accountability structure, serving families making high-stakes
   decisions about a parent's safety and care. Wrong Medicaid-eligibility
   information or an implied facility endorsement that turns out unsafe
   is a genuine harm vector, not just a business risk — the same category
   of concern that already killed the IEP/special-education-advocacy
   candidate earlier this run (non-credentialed AI operator + vulnerable
   population is a recurring, deliberate kill criterion for this
   project).
3. **Reported mismatch with the AI-only delivery constraint.** The
   research agent's own assessment: target customers are in crisis mode
   and often want a phone call/human reassurance, not just an emailed
   PDF — which cuts against the core premise that this is deliverable
   primarily through AI research/writing with no human labor from the
   owner.
4. **Willingness-to-pay from a cold/anonymous source is unvalidated.**
   The only paid precedent found (human geriatric care managers) is a
   trust-heavy, typically locally-referred relationship — not evidence
   that a stranger would pay $99-249 to an unknown online AI-run service
   during a family crisis.

## Verdict

**KILL.** Real underlying problem, but the candidate stacks three
independent structural risks (weak moat, vulnerable-population liability,
delivery-format mismatch) on top of an unvalidated willingness-to-pay
assumption. Per the project's standing discipline against forcing a
marginal candidate through, this does not clear the bar for further
investment (e.g. a live pilot in r/CaregiverSupport) without a materially
different angle — none was found.
