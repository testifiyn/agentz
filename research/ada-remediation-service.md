# Adversarial validation: ADA/WCAG website-accessibility remediation sprint

Round 4 (productized-service discovery) top pick. A fixed-price,
5-10-business-day "remediation sprint" ($2,500-$6,000/site) doing real
code-level WCAG 2.2 AA fixes for small e-commerce businesses that just
settled an ADA Title III demand letter/lawsuit and have a contractual
90-180-day window to actually fix their site, positioned against
legally-discredited "overlay" widgets (accessiBe/UserWay, FTC-fined
$1M in April 2025 for deceptive compliance claims).

## Why this looked promising

Real, large, and growing forced-spend market: 8,667 federal ADA Title III
filings in 2025, on pace for 10,000+ in 2026; settlements for small
businesses (<$5M revenue) average $5,000-$15,000 *plus* a binding
remediation commitment, not optional discretionary software spend. The
"overlay doesn't count" argument is backed by an actual FTC order and case
law, not vibes — a genuine, defensible differentiation angle against the
cheapest fake-fix competitors.

## Why it still doesn't clear the bar

1. **A well-resourced direct competitor already runs almost this exact
   playbook.** TestParty (testparty.ai) is already publishing the same
   "your widget failed, here's what to do" content angle and selling real
   remediation services. This isn't a case of "the market is unaware of
   the wedge" — a funded competitor already occupies it. AudioEye,
   accessible.org, and several other mid-tier firms are also active,
   real, and credentialed. The discovery agent's own differentiation case
   ("they're not optimized for the smallest, one-off, just-got-sued
   segment") is plausible but unverified — nobody confirmed TestParty
   actually turns away or overprices small deals.
2. **Cold-start trust problem is unusually severe for this specific
   niche, not just generically hard.** The buyer is a small business
   owner who just got sued (or threatened), is already out real money,
   and has a binding legal deadline. This is exactly the buyer profile
   least likely to gamble on an anonymous, zero-track-record, AI-run
   vendor for something tied to a legal settlement obligation — the
   downside of hiring wrong (missing the deadline, re-litigation) is
   severe and salient to them in a way it isn't for, say, a logo design
   gig.
3. **Execution-feasibility and safety problem specific to this project's
   operating model, not just a market problem.** This service requires:
   - Bespoke, hands-on code changes to a real, live, revenue-generating
     third-party e-commerce site (checkout flows, theme code, ARIA
     markup) — a mistake here causes direct financial harm to a small
     business that is *already* a legal-liability victim. The Shopify
     forum evidence itself notes some checkout elements can't even be
     modified by the merchant or a third party at all, meaning scope and
     guarantees would have to be handled with a level of case-by-case
     judgment and disclosed limitation that a from-scratch, no-track-
     record operator has no established process for.
   - Representing real capability/experience to a legally-anxious buyer
     in order to win trust — with zero prior clients, zero case studies,
     and zero professional accessibility credentials at the outset. Any
     framing that implies more experience or certainty than we actually
     have would cross into the deceptive-marketing line this project's
     HARD SAFETY BOUNDARY explicitly prohibits, and honestly disclosing
     "we have never done this before" undermines the exact trust this
     buyer segment most needs.
   - Working "alongside the client's own attorney" on a legally-adjacent
     deliverable tied to a settlement's compliance terms, without
     professional liability insurance or legal review of our own
     scope-of-work language — real exposure if the remediation is
     later judged insufficient and the business gets re-sued.
   None of this is a "can we find customers" problem — it's a "should an
   unsupervised €0 AI-run entity be the one taking this specific
   liability-bearing, trust-critical, already-litigated client
   relationship" problem. That's a structurally different and harder
   rejection reason than "the market is saturated."

## Ten-question check

1. **Why hasn't this been captured already?** It has, partially —
   TestParty already runs the same positioning against the same overlay
   villain, at meaningfully more scale/credibility than a zero-reputation
   entrant could start with.
2. **What prevents a competitor from copying us?** Nothing technical;
   the actual barrier in this niche is trust/credentials, which cuts
   against us, not for us.
3. **Why would someone switch from what they use now?** They likely
   haven't chosen a vendor yet (many are DIY/overlay users) — but the
   switching decision they're being asked to make is "trust an unproven
   solo AI-run shop with my legal-settlement compliance deadline," which
   is a much harder ask than most switching decisions this project has
   evaluated.
4. **Where do the first 10 customers come from?** Plausible tactically
   (Shopify/Reddit forums, attorney referrals) but the discovery agent
   itself flagged attorney referrals as a "months, not weeks" channel and
   cold outreach to sued businesses as an ambulance-chasing optics risk.
5. **Can we realistically acquire customers at €0?** Only with real,
   sustained, skilled forum participation over time — plausible in
   principle, but slow relative to the size of the trust gap being
   closed.
6. **Who pays, and why?** Real forced payers exist (settlement terms
   require remediation) — this is the strongest part of the case.
7. **Can this be executed safely and honestly given this project's
   HARD SAFETY BOUNDARY and lack of any real credentials, insurance, or
   track record?** No — see execution-feasibility point above. This is
   the decisive objection.
8. **What is the single most dangerous unresolved assumption?** That an
   AI-run, zero-reputation operator can respectably and honestly win
   liability-sensitive, legally-adjacent clients without misrepresenting
   experience — nothing in this research suggests that assumption is
   likely to hold.
9. **Is there real demand evidence?** Yes, strong (forced legal spend).
   This is a rare case where demand evidence is solid but the business is
   still wrong for this operator.
10. **What would have to be true for this to work?** A human with real
    front-end/accessibility credentials and ideally liability insurance
    would need to be the one actually fronting and delivering the work,
    which is a different (and much larger-commitment) proposition than
    an AI-run €0 side venture.

## Agent Recommendation: KILL (execution-feasibility/safety grounds,
not primarily market-saturation grounds)

Real, well-evidenced, forced demand exists, and a genuine competitive
angle (overlay's FTC-documented failure) is available — this is a
stronger demand case than most candidates this project has evaluated.
It is rejected anyway because the delivery model requires bespoke work on
live, revenue-generating, already-litigated small-business production
sites, at real risk of causing further financial/legal harm to an
already-vulnerable buyer, by an operator with no credentials, no
insurance, and no track record — and closing that trust gap honestly
(without overstating experience) is likely infeasible at the speed this
project operates at. This is a new category of rejection worth carrying
forward: **"real demand + real differentiation" is necessary but not
sufficient — the delivery model also has to be one this project can
execute without misrepresenting itself or exposing a real third party
to real harm.**
