# Content/curation business model — discovery round (2026-10-07)

**Status: KILLED — 0 of 17 candidates survived.**

## Why this round was run

After 11 straight software-product discovery strategies failed (see
`MEMORY.md` and `ideas/candidates.md`), the prior run's recommendation
flagged "business-model change — productized service, content/newsletter,
or community model" as the one untested lever. This round tested the
content/curation half of that recommendation (a tightly-niched newsletter
or curated resource requiring ongoing manual verification judgment). A
parallel round tested the productized-service half — see
`research/grant-writing-fractional-nonprofits.md` for its one survivor.

## Method

17 candidate niches/angles tested across regulatory-compliance,
professional-services, creative-gig, and funding/grants categories. For
each: searched for existing newsletters/creators, checked real traction
signals (subscriber counts, platform scale, publication longevity), and
checked whether the audience's info need is already adequately served.

## Full candidate list and kill reasons

1. **EPR packaging-law compliance newsletter (small/mid CPG brands)** —
   KILLED. Already redundantly served free by a 40-year trade magazine
   (Resource Recycling's "Policy Now"), Industry Dive's Packaging Dive,
   a law firm's client newsletter (King & Spalding's "All Wrapped Up"),
   and a 50,000+-subscriber sustainability newsletter (Trellis Briefing).
2. **Tariff-change newsletter for small importers/DTC brands** — KILLED.
   An existing solo-operator paid weekly newsletter (Tariff Tracker, on
   Buttondown) already occupies this exact format/audience, with no
   evidence of stalling.
3. **CBAM (EU carbon border tax) newsletter for SME importers** — KILLED.
   A new exemption threshold is legislating ~182,000 SME importers out of
   scope entirely; remaining large importers already served by Big 4 tax
   advisory newsletters.
4. **EU e-invoicing/VAT mandate newsletter for small e-commerce** —
   KILLED. EY runs a recurring "e-Invoicing Monthly Newsletter"; multiple
   e-invoicing SaaS vendors run this as lead-gen content with a direct
   incentive to publish compulsively.
5. **Non-dilutive funding/grants newsletter for EU climate-tech
   startups** — KILLED. A direct newsletter competitor already runs
   monthly issues; two funded startups (Streamline Climate, Open Grants)
   sell exactly this as their core product.
6. **Short-term rental (Airbnb) city-regulation tracker/newsletter** —
   KILLED. Dense SaaS-vendor content marketing already covers this
   (BNB Calc, Touchstay, Hospitable).
7. **Self-storage operator industry newsletter** — KILLED outright.
   Inside Self-Storage has run a free, targeted weekly newsletter for
   facility operators for years.
8. **Commercial drone (BVLOS/Part 108) regulation newsletter for small
   operators** — KILLED. Two large monetized incumbent communities
   (UAV Coach: 100k+ members, Drone U: 25k+ pilots trained) already run
   newsletters with sponsor relationships on this exact audience/topic.
9. **US state privacy-law tracker/newsletter for small businesses** —
   KILLED immediately. IAPP (a well-funded professional association)
   already runs a free, weekly-updated tracker plus a dedicated digest.
10. **Independent-pharmacy PBM-reimbursement/closure crisis newsletter**
    — KILLED, most "owned" niche tested. NCPA (22,000+ member trade
    association) bundles newsletters + magazine + legislative tracking as
    a baseline membership benefit, backed by lobbying infrastructure.
11. **Therapist private-practice insurance-credentialing newsletter** —
    KILLED (multiply saturated, not single-incumbent). At least 4
    overlapping solo newsletters/communities already run in this space,
    plus two VC-funded platforms (Alma, Headway) monetizing the underlying
    pain directly via free educational content as acquisition marketing.
12. **Reshoring-manufacturing newsletter for small US manufacturers** —
    KILLED. An established nonprofit industry org (Reshoring Initiative)
    already runs a recurring "Reshore Newsletter."
13. **AI voice-cloning disruption newsletter for freelance voice actors**
    — KILLED (weakest kill of the set, no pure neutral solo incumbent,
    but three platform-run newsletters/content hubs with far larger reach
    — Voice123, Voices.com, VO Buzz Weekly — already serve this exact
    audience/topic with direct monetization incentive).
14. **Freelance-translator AI-disruption newsletter** — KILLED on strong
    prior evidence. Slator is a dominant, well-resourced industry-
    intelligence operation; ATA runs a member newsletter; a 15+ year
    established blog (Corinne McKay's "Training For Translators") already
    owns the freelancer-specific angle. Also overlaps the already-killed
    "non-English regulated professions" vein from the original 11
    software-discovery strategies.
15. **Cannabis dispensary state-regulation compliance newsletter for
    small operators** — KILLED. MJBizDaily is an established, large paid
    trade publication with continuous compliance coverage. A narrower
    ops-level angle for single-location dispensaries was considered but
    rejected on liability (regulatory guidance in a criminally-exposed
    industry, non-lawyer solo curator), regulator-self-publishing, and
    cannabis advertising-restriction grounds.
16. **DTC wine-shipping compliance newsletter for small wineries** —
    KILLED. Sovos ShipCompliant (serves 2,000+ paying wineries) produces
    this content at scale as lead-gen; WineAmerica runs "Compliance
    Matters" as a member resource.
17. **Tariff/customs newsletter for Kickstarter/crowdfunding hardware
    creators** — KILLED, cleanest kill of all. Kickstarter itself
    publishes official guidance directly to creators and built a
    first-party "Tariff Manager" tool; the platform that owns the
    relationship already is the newsletter.

## Synthesis

Every single niche had an incumbent already serving the audience, falling
into one of six recurring institutional types: (1) a trade association
bundling it as a membership benefit, (2) a law/Big 4 firm running it as
client-relationship marketing, (3) a VC-funded or bootstrapped vendor
publishing it as acquisition marketing for a paid product that solves the
same pain, (4) the platform itself that owns the actual relationship, (5)
an established trade-media outlet already on the beat, or (6) an earlier
solo creator who already occupied the niche 1-3+ years ago. A seventh
cross-cutting finding: programmatic AI-generated SEO content (one vendor,
beancount.io, was observed flooding tariff/e-invoicing/reshoring/drone
search results with auto-translated content across a dozen languages)
means even where no dedicated newsletter competitor exists, the organic/
SEO distribution channel a new newsletter would depend on is already being
actively polluted.

**This extends the project's core structural finding (from the 11 failed
software strategies) to the content/curation business model**: anything
discoverable by one solo builder via web search is discoverable by
institutional incumbents too — and in content specifically, those
incumbents (trade associations, law firms, platforms) have *longer*
tenure and *larger* resources than a solo newsletter operator, and their
moats (membership dues, direct platform relationships, decades of trust)
are structurally harder to erode than a software product's moat is to
clone. If anything, content/curation is a worse fit than software for a
€0 solo operator working from pure desk research, not a better one.

## Recommendation

Do not pursue content/curation as this project's business model. Treat
this lever as tested and closed (not to be re-tried without a materially
different angle — e.g., an angle sourced from the operator's own
pre-existing expertise/network rather than from web-search discovery,
which this round's method cannot by itself distinguish as viable or not).
