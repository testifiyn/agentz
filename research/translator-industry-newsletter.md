# Candidate: "Translator/Interpreter AI-Disruption Intelligence" newsletter

**Status: KILLED** (2026-10-02)

## Problem

Freelance translators are living through a sudden, severe income collapse
from LLM-based machine translation, compounded by a specific dishonest
practice: agencies posting "translation" jobs on ProZ.com that are
actually disguised AI-training/data-labeling gigs at exploitative rates.
Hypothesis: no trusted, frequently-updated, freelancer-facing source
tracks which agencies are legitimate vs. AI-training traps, real
crowdsourced rates by language pair, and which specialties remain
defensibly human.

## Evidence for the problem (real, strong)

- CNN-reported case: a 15-year veteran translator's income fell from six
  figures to ~€8,000 in 2025.
- Quebec-based translator reporting a 60% income decline in 2024, with an
  80% drop projected by end of 2025.
- ATA president Andy Benzo and UK Society of Authors' Ian Giles both told
  press that translators are leaving the profession entirely.
- Active 6+ page ProZ.com forum thread, "About the constant AI training
  job posts" — sustained, specific community anger, not a one-off
  complaint.
- Adjacent paid precedent: Slator (paid language-industry intelligence)
  and ATA's dues-gated Chronicle prove people already pay for curated
  intelligence in this space generally.

## Why it was killed

Dedicated adversarial validation (2026-10-02) found the proposed
differentiator does not hold up:

1. **ProZ.com is already building the specific missing piece.** ProZ
   staff (Monica Oliveira) has taken the forum thread's own proposals to
   ProZ's product team: a "fair market badge" on job posts, and expanding
   ProZ's existing, live **Community Rates tool** (crowdsourced rates by
   language pair, already built from member-submitted data) to add
   rate-by-task-type categories — including AI-training/data-labeling —
   so underpriced or disguised postings could be flagged. This is
   substantially the same product the candidate proposed to build from
   zero, except ProZ has an existing userbase, login/trust graph, and
   moderation apparatus already in place.
2. **Informal crowdsourcing of this exact data already exists** in
   parallel: the ProZ forum thread itself functions as a de facto
   tracking/discussion location; a r/TranslationStudies thread already
   proposed and executed "let's all share our real earnings"; third-party
   blacklists exist (TranslationDirectory.com lists 400+ "untrustworthy"
   outsourcers; older ProZ threads list 80+ blacklisted agencies). The
   gap is "nobody has built a polished product around this data," not
   "nobody is crowdsourcing this data" — a thin wedge, not an open field.
3. **Defamation/liability risk is real, not hypothetical**, demonstrated
   by the incumbent's own experience: a multi-page ProZ thread titled
   "Are negative Blue Board postings defamatory?" documents ProZ (a
   25+-year-old company with legal staff) removing/restoring Blue Board
   entries under pressure from disputing agencies. A zero-capital,
   unaccountable newsletter publicly naming specific agencies as
   exploitative or AI-training-in-disguise carries materially higher
   exposure than the well-resourced incumbent already treats carefully.
4. **Willingness to pay is deteriorating, not available.** Multiple
   sources show the target population's income collapsing 60-80%;
   post-editing rates have halved (€0.04 → €0.02/word); ~43% of UK
   translators report income declines from generative AI (Society of
   Authors). A population mid-collapse wants free crowdsourced
   information — which is exactly what it is already building for free —
   not a new paid subscription.

## Verdict

**KILL.** No narrower angle survives: the specific missing piece is
already being built by a better-resourced, better-trusted incumbent in
parallel with existing informal community crowdsourcing, the liability
exposure is demonstrably real even for that incumbent, and the target
market's ability to pay is actively worsening.

## Pattern note

This is the second time (after the nomad-visa tracker) this project has
found a "we'll crowdsource/keep it current, incumbents are static" wedge
collapse under dedicated adversarial re-check once a specific competitor
mechanism was checked directly rather than assumed absent. Standing
lesson: always check whether the obvious incumbent already has a
half-built version of the exact proposed differentiator before treating
a gap as open.
