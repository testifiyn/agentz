# Candidate: Fractional/retainer grant writing for small nonprofits

**Status: MARGINAL SURVIVOR — only candidate to survive discovery across 13
strategies tried to date. Not a clean win. Proceeding to deeper validation,
not straight to build.**

## Origin

Found in the "Round 12" productized-service discovery pass (2026-10-07),
run specifically to test a business-model pivot (service, not software)
after 11 straight software-product discovery strategies failed. Full
discovery context and the 11 other candidates tested in that same pass
(all killed) are in `ideas/candidates.md`.

## Problem

Small-to-mid nonprofits (roughly $200k-$3M annual budget) need grant
proposals written on an ongoing basis to fund programs, but are too big to
rely on volunteer labor and too small to justify a full-time grant
professional (~$60k+/year salary). Grant writing is a real, chronic,
recurring need, not a one-off problem.

## Customer

Executive directors / development leads at small nonprofits, specifically
ones without a "Development Director" on staff (a screenable proxy via
LinkedIn / org staff pages) but with an active grants calendar.

## Evidence

- Grant writing is a professionalized freelance career with its own trade
  body (Grant Professionals Association, which sets ethical norms —
  e.g. explicitly banning commission-based pay structures).
- Real, active rate cards exist and are being paid today: Upwork grant
  writers at $35-60/hr (up to $125/hr for advanced writers); flat fees of
  $200-500 for a small foundation grant, $1,500-5,000 per foundation
  proposal, $5,000-15,000+ for federal applications (golance.com,
  grantable.co).
- The underlying task resists full AI commoditization better than most of
  the killed candidates: multiple sources note grant reviewers "can spot
  generic AI language a mile off" and that it actively hurts applications
  — meaning cheap AI grant tools (Grantable, Instrumentl, Granted AI)
  existing doesn't eliminate the premium for genuinely well-researched,
  funder-specific human writing the way it did for (e.g.) chargeback
  rebuttals or review replies.

## Frequency / Severity

Recurring (nonprofits apply to multiple grants per year on an ongoing
cycle) and high-stakes (grant funding is often existential for program
continuation).

## Current solution / Existing spending

Three segments, bimodal:
1. Smallest orgs (volunteer fire departments, small rescues, PTAs) use
   **unpaid volunteers** sourced via Idealist.org — confirmed live unpaid
   listings exist. This segment will not pay. Do not target it.
2. The target segment (not-tiny, not-big) pays freelancers directly via
   Upwork/Thumbtack at the rates above, or through the OpenGrants
   marketplace.
3. Larger orgs hire in-house grant professionals (~$60k+/yr) — not this
   candidate's target.

## Competitors

Upwork, Thumbtack, and OpenGrants already organize supply and demand for
this exact service. This is a known, mature gig category — not a hidden
gap. A new entrant is joining an existing trade, not discovering empty
space.

## Competitor weaknesses / Opportunity

Existing supply is fragmented freelancers at commodity marketplaces, not a
branded, retainer-based "fractional grant writer" offering. The
opportunity is less "undiscovered demand" and more "an underserved
packaging of known demand" — turning one-off gig work into a recurring
monthly retainer relationship, which better fits a sustainable small
business than one-off freelance gigs.

## Differentiation

Not structural — it's skill, research quality, and relationship-based.
This is explicitly *not* a moat that resists a competitor "reading the
landing page and hiring their own freelancer" the way the brief asked to
test for. It only holds if the operator is actually good at research-heavy
persuasive writing, sustained over a real relationship. Honest assessment:
weak moat, consistent with being a real trade rather than a discovered
wedge.

## Distribution (first 10 / 100 / 1,000 — see important caveat below)

- First 10: Nonprofit Happy Hour Facebook group (~40,000 members, active,
  fundraising-focused), r/nonprofit, r/AskNonprofit, "Grant Writers Unite"
  Facebook group, state/regional nonprofit association member directories
  (every US state has one; filter by org size + absence of a "Development
  Director" title on LinkedIn), OpenGrants marketplace as secondary lead
  source.
- First 100: referral-driven once 2-3 funded-grant proof points exist
  (nonprofit sector is relationship/reference-dense); state association
  directories scaled up.
- First 1,000: not modeled — this is a relationship-sold, low-volume-
  per-operator service (more retainer clients ≠ linear scaling without
  hiring more writers), so "1,000 customers" isn't the right scale unit
  for this business model. Scaling would mean either raising retainer
  price/quality tier or eventually subcontracting to other writers
  (team-building), not pure customer-count growth.

**Important caveat the research explicitly flagged:** every "how to
start" source found (r/nonprofit, technicalwriterhq, writermag) converges
on doing 2-3 unpaid/pro-bono proposals first to build a portfolio before
anyone will pay. Realistic time to first PAID client is weeks-to-months,
not days — this sits awkwardly against this project's normal "first 10
customers at €0, fast" framing, and against the bootstrapping-loop
preference for real paid revenue as the strongest evidence tier.

## Monetization

$300-500 flat-fee single proposals initially (to build 3-5 portfolio
pieces; some unpaid/discounted realistic for the first 1-2), transitioning
to $1,000-2,500/month retainers once 2-3 funded-grant proof points exist.

## €0 feasibility

Yes for outreach/acquisition (all channels above are free). Not €0 for
time — the "2-3 unpaid proposals first" norm means real unpaid labor
before any revenue, which is a cost even without a cash outlay.

## Risks

- **Execution risk dominates, not demand risk** — this is the key
  difference from the 12 killed candidates, most of which failed because
  the market itself didn't exist or was already fully served at a lower
  price than a human could sustain. Here the market and willingness-to-pay
  are real and current; the open question is whether the operator can
  actually write competitively and build enough trust to win against
  established Upwork freelancers with track records.
- Slow, credibility-gated, reference-driven sale into a budget-constrained
  buyer.
- No structural moat — purely execution/relationship based, meaning a
  competitor absolutely could reach parity if they're also a strong writer.
- Thin per-operator scalability (addressed above).

## Fatal assumptions requiring real-world testing before further
investment

1. **The operator (human) can produce genuinely strong, funder-specific,
   non-generic-sounding grant proposals.** This cannot be validated by
   more desk research — it requires either a real portfolio sample or a
   trial unpaid/discounted proposal with a real nonprofit and an honest
   look at whether it would plausibly win funding.
2. Nonprofits in the target directories will actually respond to free-tier
   outreach (DMs/posts in the Facebook groups / subreddits) rather than
   only working through paid marketplaces they already trust (Upwork).
3. The "too big for volunteers, too small to hire" segment is reachable at
   meaningful volume through the proposed free channels, not just a
   handful of leads.

## Recommendation

Do not build anything yet. This survived discovery screening only in the
qualified sense that it's a real, evidence-backed market with proven
spending — not that it's a clean, defensible, fast-to-first-revenue
opportunity. Per this project's standing discipline (don't force a weak
idea through, and real customer evidence outranks AI reasoning), the next
step is a cheap, direct test of fatal assumption #1 above — see
`MEMORY.md` for the specific next action — before committing further time
to this path, and before treating it as a locked `ACTIVE_BUSINESS`.
