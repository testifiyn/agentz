# Medicaid/SNAP policy-change digest for frontline case managers — KILLED

**Date:** 2026-09-24
**Verdict:** KILL as originally scoped. A much narrower conditional version
was identified but not picked (see below) — the shrunken scope, combined
with the liability and monetization findings, means it does not clear this
project's bar for a confident GO.

## Candidate as proposed

A weekly digest newsletter curating Medicaid/SNAP eligibility rule changes
for frontline case managers and benefits navigators (county eligibility
workers, nonprofit navigators, community health workers, 211 staff),
translating scattered official guidance into "what changed this week and
what it means for your caseload." Surfaced by a content/newsletter
discovery pass (Round 4) as the one real survivor out of 6 niches screened,
against the backdrop of major 2026 federal eligibility changes (OBBBA SNAP
work requirements, Medicaid work requirements effective Dec 31 2026,
six-month re-verification, non-citizen eligibility restrictions).

## Adversarial validation findings

**1. The competitor gap is real but much smaller than framed.** Every
state Medicaid/SNAP agency already runs a mandatory internal bulletin/
listserv system pushing "what changed" directly to its own eligibility
staff (confirmed for WI, MN, TX, MI; the pattern is universal across state
HHS agencies). That covers roughly half the originally-stated audience —
state/county-employed eligibility workers — for free, automatically, from
the entity with actual legal authority. The addressable audience shrinks
to the non-agency-employed half: nonprofit navigators, CHWs, 211 staff —
exactly the segment with the least organizational budget and the least
standing to treat an unaccredited third party as authoritative.
Precedent exists for the format working: CLINIC's "Topline Immigration
Policy Updates" serves a comparable practitioner audience — but it's run
by an established, credentialed nonprofit with subject-matter legal staff,
not a solo AI-assisted generalist.

**2. Liability is the strongest kill signal.** A "not legal/eligibility
advice" disclaimer reduces legal exposure but does not prevent the
predictable practical failure mode: a caseworker under caseload pressure
acting on a well-written summary without re-checking the primary source —
that behavior is the entire value proposition of the format, not an edge
case. Given real 50-state variation in rollout timing, a wrong effective
date or state mix-up is plausible, not a tail risk, and the downstream
harm (a family losing SNAP/Medicaid, a missed appeal window) is often
irreversible within a benefit period, falling on people with no cushion.
This is categorically worse than a typical SaaS bug, and unlike comparable
law-firm newsletters, a solo €0 no-credential operator has no named/
licensed author, no institutional backing, and no insurance backstop.

**3. Monetization is a hope-based play, not an evidence-based one.**
Propel's sponsorship product targets SNAP recipients, not caseworkers —
not a natural fit. findhelp/Unite Us sell to agency/CBO leadership, not
frontline readers. Realistic sponsor interest starts around 1,000-2,500
engaged subscribers per general newsletter benchmarks — meaning months of
unpaid growth before any sponsorship conversation is even credible. The
one funded precedent in this space (NCOA, CLINIC) is grant/foundation-
funded through an established nonprofit, not solo-bootstrapped. This is
structurally "build audience for 6-12+ months with zero revenue, hope to
monetize later," which conflicts with this project's own evidence-based
(not hope-based) operating discipline (Section 30/31: "I'd probably pay"
is not "I paid" — here we don't even have the former).

**4. Execution isn't sustainable at full scope.** True 50-state weekly
coverage by a solo, non-credentialed, AI-assisted operator doesn't meet
the accuracy bar the topic demands. A federal + top-5-state MVP is more
tractable but serves a minority of any national navigator audience,
undermining the must-subscribe value proposition for most readers.

## Conditional-GO scope considered and rejected

The validating agent proposed a narrower version: retarget exclusively to
non-agency navigators, never restate specific dates/numbers in body copy
(link every claim to the primary source, curate/contextualize only), run
3+ months fully manual and unpaid through 2-3 navigator associations
before writing any code, and defer all monetization planning until reach
is proven.

This is not being picked up. Reasons: even in the narrower scope, the
publication still curates information vulnerable people's benefits depend
on, from an unaccredited solo source, so the core liability concern is
reduced but not eliminated. Combined with a shrunken audience, an
unproven and possibly nonexistent monetization path, and a direct
institutional precedent (CLINIC) suggesting this format works because of
institutional backing this project cannot claim — the risk-adjusted case
is weak enough that pursuing it would mean forcing a marginal idea through
to show activity, which is exactly the failure mode this project's
operating principles warn against.

## What this adds to the standing pattern

Round 4 tested three axes deliberately chosen to escape the "AI clones
software in weeks" problem that killed 11/11 prior candidates: fresh
triggers (0 survivors), productized services (0 survivors, with a clean
structural bind: trust-moated services can't bootstrap at €0, and
€0-bootstrappable services have no moat), and content/newsletter (1
survivor at discovery, killed at validation on liability + monetization +
shrunken-audience grounds). All three axes are now exhausted for this
round. See `MEMORY.md` for the synthesized read and recommended next
steps.
